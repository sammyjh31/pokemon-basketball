# Game flow: playoffs, timeouts, referees, home gyms and rest

Add versioned game-flow rules (`gameFlowVersion: 1`) to every newly started solo challenge and local league, and to existing franchises and local careers at the next season boundary. Active seasons, archives and historical games keep their engine. Requires `courtVersion: 1`. No manual controls; trainer coaches act automatically.

Simulated games today play the same way whether it is game 23 or Game 7 of the Finals, on a back-to-back or after three days off, under any whistle and in any building. Game flow adds that context without synthetic score bonuses. Every effect works through existing basketball levers: minutes, energy, play and shooter selection, foul rates, shot log-odds that the court engine already applies, and species abilities.

## Fixture context

Each scheduled game carries a frozen `GameFlowFixture`, stored on fixtures and game summaries the same way chemistry and morale snapshots are:

- `stage`: `regular` or `playoff`. Playoff games also record `round`, `game` (1-based), `homeWins`, `awayWins` and `winsNeeded`.
- `rest`: days off before the game for each team (0 = back-to-back, capped at 3).
- `trip`: consecutive road games for each team, including this one (0 at home, capped at 6).

Rest comes from a deterministic calendar, not extra random draws. Regular-season round `r` spans days `2r` and `2r + 1`. A fixture is played on the second day when a salted hash of its seed falls below 22%. A team's rest is its game day minus its previous game day minus one, so about one game in six is a back-to-back (about 14 per 82-game season). The first game has 3 days of rest. Playoff games always have at least one day of rest; series openers have two.

The referee crew and home arena are derived inside the engine from the game seed and home club, so they need no storage. Both appear in the game report.

## Playoff basketball

Playoff games use stage-aware coaching and intensity:

- Tighter rotations. Coaches move minutes from the end of the bench to the core: the 8th player's target falls by 40%, the 7th by 12%, and starters absorb the difference up to 42 minutes. The shift scales by `1 − 0.5 × rest preference`, so deep-rotation coaches tighten less. Elimination games tighten 30% more.
- Slower, half-court play. Offense takes half a second longer to advance, transition chances fall 10%, and every shot has 0.02 lower log-odds for both teams (tighter defense, like the real drop in playoff efficiency).
- Stars carry more. Handler and shooter selection weights rise for the team's top creators (exponent 3 → 3.6 on usage-driven selection).
- Pressure. The clutch composure term is 1.5× as strong (an extra `(composure − 60) × 0.00125` log-odds). In elimination games every shot adds `(composure − 60) × 0.0017` log-odds, about ±0.04 percentage points per composure point.
- Home crowd. Home shot log-odds rise from 0.045 to 0.06.

On court rules v2, the audit measures the combined effect at about 4.5 fewer points and 3.7 fewer possessions per team than the regular season. The 8th player's minutes fall from about 11 to 7 in Game 3 and 6 in Game 7.
- Series adjustments. A coach who lost the previous game of the series, with adaptability of at least 0.5, re-plans the defense from the series' scouting memory: switch against three-heavy opponents, zone against rim-heavy ones. A coach who lost by 15 or more also tries the roster's second-best tactic.
- The whistle loosens: referee tightness shifts down by half a step.

## Coach timeouts

Each team has six regulation timeouts, at most three in the fourth quarter, plus two per overtime period. Only the team about to gain possession can call one, so the timeout also sets up an after-timeout (ATO) play. Timeouts use no game clock and no random draws. Coaches call them for three reasons:

1. Stop a run. The opponent has scored at least `10 − 4 × adaptability` unanswered points, and the coach keeps two timeouts in reserve for the last three minutes.
2. Rest. Floor energy is below `56 + 10 × rest preference`, and the next substitution window is more than 20 seconds away.
3. Late game. In the final 24 seconds of the fourth quarter or overtime, the team trails by 1–3 points or is tied and has the ball.

A timeout recovers 3 energy for players on the floor and 1 for the bench. The coach re-evaluates scheme and lineup immediately instead of waiting for the next minute. The ATO possession favors the coach's best available play type. In the late-game case it also advances the ball (1 second instead of a full backcourt advance) and runs the play for the floor's best clutch option (composure plus shooting). An ATO shot gains 0.06 log-odds for the designed look.

## Referee crews

A salted hash of the seed picks one of five crews: Let them play (−1), Loose (−0.5), Standard (0), Tight (+0.5) or Whistle-happy (+1). Tightness `t` scales foul rates:

- Reach fouls ×(1 + 0.2t). Shooting fouls ×(1 + 0.12t). Charges and illegal screens ×(1 + 0.15t).
- The draw-foul versus discipline slope ×(1 + 0.4t), so disciplined defenders and foul-drawing scorers matter more under tight crews.

Playoff crews shift down half a step (capped at −1).

## Home gyms and field conditions

Each club's arena has a field condition: rain, sun, sand, snow, electric, grassy, misty, psychic or neutral. CPU clubs take theirs from the club identity (Cerulean Waves → rain, Cinnabar Flames → sun, Mahogany Frost → snow, Rustboro Rock → sand and so on). Club 0's arena follows its roster: the weather type shared by at least three of its eight players, otherwise neutral. While a weather-setting ability (Drizzle, Drought, Sand Stream, Snow Warning or a terrain surge) is on the floor, that player's condition replaces the arena's. A home setter takes priority over an away setter.

Matching conditions strengthen existing species abilities, with their normal effect unchanged elsewhere:

- Weather speed abilities (Swift Swim, Chlorophyll, Sand Rush, Surge Surfer, Protosynthesis, Quark Drive): transition bonus 2.5 → 3.5 points, movement 1.08 → 1.12.
- Weather endurance abilities (Hydration, Dry Skin, Ice Body): energy drain 0.82 → 0.75.
- Weather evasion abilities (Sand Veil, Snow Cloak): contested-shot effect −1.5 → −2.5 points.
- Sand Force: midrange bonus 2.5 → 3.5 points in sand. Leaf Guard: contested-jumper bonus +1 point in sun.

Type comfort changes energy drain only, never accuracy. Comfortable types drain 5% slower: Water in rain, Fire and Grass in sun, Rock, Ground and Steel in sand, Ice in snow, Electric in electric, Grass in grassy, Fairy in misty, Psychic in psychic. Uncomfortable types drain 4% faster: Fire in rain, Water and Ice in sun, Dragon in misty.

## Rest and back-to-backs

On a back-to-back, each player starts below full energy: `(2.5 + 5 × planned minutes / 36) × (1.25 − stamina / 200)`, about 7 for a 34-minute starter with average stamina. In the audit, one team on a back-to-back loses roughly 2–3 percentage points of win probability. Each road game beyond the third adds 0.5, up to 1.5. Two or more days of rest start everyone fresh.

Coaches with a rest preference of at least 0.6 manage load on back-to-backs: each starter's target drops by up to `4 × rest preference` minutes, and the bench absorbs it. Other coaches keep the normal plan and accept tired starters.

## Reporting

`LeagueMatch.gameFlow` records the stage, crew, arena condition and changes from setters, rest, trip, the coaches' playoff adjustments and every timeout (team, period, clock, reason, energy before, score). Gameplay never reads the report. Box scores show it as **Game context**, including a table of timeouts.

## Compatibility

All version-absent output stays byte-identical. Flag-absent games make the same random draws in the same order. Old saves, archives, official played games and the arcade keep their rules. Played arcade games ignore game flow in this version.
