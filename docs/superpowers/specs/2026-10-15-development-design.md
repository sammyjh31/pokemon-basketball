# Franchise player development and CPU transactions

The user wants living multi-season leagues: playing time and performance can improve or reduce players' basketball skills, with **large swings and frequent surprises**, and other clubs can trade with each other. Keep eight players per club, 240 unique Gen I–II species, career identity, original-team honors and all existing saves. No aging, evolution, contracts, free agency or forced trades involving the user's club.

Development runs once per completed offseason, after archiving results and before CPU trades. It uses regular-season minutes, per-minute impact and skill-specific production, a deterministic seasonal form event, and regression of accumulated development to limit runaway ratings. Skill changes are whole points, at most -10/+12 annually and -30/+30 cumulatively; final basketball skills remain 0–100. Frequent breakout/slump events affect multiple skills. Factual Pokémon data never changes. Summaries and advanced traits derive from developed skills. Reports record all 240 players, before/after best-position rating, minutes, impact, individual skill changes and reasons.

CPU clubs search for up to six mutually beneficial one-for-one swaps, at most one per CPU club each offseason. Neither party can be the user. Both sides evaluate current developed ability and resulting roster fit. No trade is forced when no acceptable pair exists. Development follows the species when traded; transactions record both clubs and their reasoning in the same chronological log as user trades.

Keep raw archive/next-year helpers compatible with existing fixtures. Reducer entry into the offseason archives then processes it, and starting the next year ensures processing has happened. A pre-existing offseason save can explicitly run its missing offseason once; the UI asks the player to review development and CPU activity before advancing. Repeated actions/reloads never reroll or duplicate effects. The completed season retains its original rating inputs and replayable boxes throughout its offseason. On advancing, the existing archive retains summaries, rosters and honors; full historical box browsing remains outside this change.

New cumulative skill offsets live on each LeagueTeam keyed by its eight species; all 30 developed teams carry this field, including empty offsets. New fixed-roster results are model 5; no-offset model 3/4 paths remain numerically identical. Playoff model 1 operates on the frozen league inputs and remains unchanged for prior saves. Franchise model 2 adds offseason reports and generalized CPU trade metadata; model 1 loads without retroactive development. Active seasons replay using their saved development. Validate offset bounds/keys/ownership, report uniqueness and accounting, chronological trades and career consistency; preserve damaged source bytes.

UI: an offseason report tab with year/team/search filters, gains/declines, expandable skill/reason details and CPU transaction feed; updated roster/court/bench/trade/scouting ratings reflect development. History and career stats keep their existing meaning. Test old/new save round trips, deterministic game/box replay, stale caches, both-direction growth, development after trades, duplicate processing, uniqueness, CPU fairness and mobile layout. Commit and push main under the user's standing authorization.
