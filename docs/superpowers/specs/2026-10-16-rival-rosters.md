# Rival-aware roster construction

Use completed type-tactics games to inform rookie drafts and trade negotiations across seasons, in standard franchises and local careers. Retain basketball quality, legal roster sizes, human control and deterministic saves. Initial drafts have no game evidence; their existing selection behavior stays intact.

At season completion, archive a compact version-1 rival report for each club: its three most important opponents, encounter/loss/playoff counts, and up to three productive scoring threats with observed attempts, field-goal points and zones. Playoff encounters receive extra priority. Include the final series game; preserve authoritative played results. Existing archives without reports retain their old decision behavior. Rich archive replays verify the new report against reconstructed matches; compact histories validate bounded data and roster ownership.

Recruiting uses only the latest three completed years (weights 1, 0.6, 0.36). Opponents and observed threats must still be on the rival's current roster; departed threats lose influence. Weight confidence by real sample size. Evaluate attacking coverage and resistance against observed threats, giving diminishing returns to redundant counters through the best two options per threat. Compare the whole roster before and after a transaction, capped to a three-point strategic bonus or penalty. Talent and rotation-loss floors remain binding. Human managers still choose picks and approve local trades.

Both clubs evaluate their own learned needs. CPU trade shortlists and final negotiations use the strategy; proposals are rechecked against current rosters as other clubs trade. Rookie candidate shortlists include the bounded strategic gain before final basketball-fit evaluation. Trade profiles, trade factors and rookie suggestions explain the named rival and threat behind a preference. No hidden win bonus or future-season information is used.

New fields are optional and versioned on annual archives, so older saves do not acquire fictional scouting. Annual reports stay compact; gameplay scouting and season replay rules do not change. Test learning from completed results, recency, opponent isolation, roster turnover, redundant coverage, talent floors, both-party negotiation, deterministic drafting and CPU trades, old saves, strict malformed data and archive replay.
